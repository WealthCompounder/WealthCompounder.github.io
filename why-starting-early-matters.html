Why Starting Early Matters More Than Timing the Market

12 August 2026 4 min read WealthCompounder

Most first-time investors spend an enormous amount of energy trying to find the "right" moment to start a SIP — waiting for a market dip, a bonus payout, or simply more confidence. It's an understandable instinct. It's also, more often than not, a costly one.

Compounding is not primarily a story about returns. It's a story about time. Money that has been invested for eighteen years does more work than money invested for twelve, almost regardless of which twelve-month window produced the better returns along the way.

The arithmetic of a head start

Consider two investors. One starts a monthly SIP at age 25 and stops contributing at 35 — ten years of investing, then nothing added after that. The other starts at 35 and contributes every month until 60 — twenty-five years of contributions. Assuming both simply let their money grow, the first investor, despite contributing for a fraction of the time, often ends up with a comparable or larger corpus by 60. The extra decade of compounding at the start outweighs fifteen additional years of contributions later.

This is why the specific month you begin matters far less than the fact that you begin.

What this means in practice

  • Small and early beats large and late. A modest SIP started today has a longer runway than a larger one started three years from now.
  • Waiting for certainty is expensive. There is rarely a moment that feels obviously "safe" to start — and every year of waiting is a year of compounding you don't get back.
  • Consistency does the rest. Once started, the discipline of continuing month after month matters more than trying to pick the best fund or the best week to invest.
The best time to start a SIP was several years ago. The second-best time is this month.

None of this means timing is irrelevant, or that every scheme is equally suitable for every goal — the right structure still depends on your time horizon, risk appetite, and what the money is ultimately for. But for someone deciding whether to start now or wait for a "better" moment, the honest answer is almost always: start now, and let time do what it does best.
